Token Architecture & Monetary Convention

What is the Haitian Dollar (HTD)?

The Haitian Dollar has existed since 1912 as an informal unit of account. HTD is a digital receipt for the Haitian Dollar — 1 HTD = 5 HTG, anchored to the convention Haitians have used for over a century.

Historical Origin

The Currency That Existed in Mind, Now Formatted for Modern Rails

When Haiti linked the gourde to the US dollar at 5:1 in 1912, ordinary market participants began quoting prices in "Haitian Dollars" to simplify daily calculations. Although no physical Haitian Dollar banknote was ever printed by the central bank, informal trade and pricing across Haiti continue to be carried out in Haitian Dollars to this day.

HTD formalizes this convention: 1 HTD always represents 5 Haitian Gourdes (HTG). By building this standard into smart contracts, buyers and sellers settle in the exact denomination they speak.

"All informal trade is carried out in Haitian Dollars. Prices are quoted in Haitian Dollars. Money is counted as Haitian Dollars." — Dr. Erin B. Taylor

Workflow

How HTD Works in Practice

A frictionless path from diaspora remittance to local vendor settlement.

1

Buy With Your Phone

Users or diaspora senders deposit funds through standard mobile channels to receive HTD at the native 5:1 gourde conversion.

2

Send & Settle Instantly

Recipients transfer HTD peer-to-peer or pay participating merchants in seconds via QR code, quoted directly in traditional Haitian Dollars. Free 0% protocol fee keeps velocity high.

3

Redeem for Cash Anytime

When paper currency is needed, users redeem their balance for physical gourdes at authorized agents. A 3% bridge fee applies on redemption (e.g. redeeming 100 HTD yields 485 HTG; 15 HTG retained to protect the reserve float).
